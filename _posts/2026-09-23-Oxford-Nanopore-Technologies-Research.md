# Research on Oxford Nanopore Technologies

Roughly 50 years ago, the technologies to read genetic code were developed. This was known as Sanger sequencing, named after and developed by Frederick Sanger. Now, in the present time, sequencing technologies have expanded and grown exponentially, with many companies worldwide using sequencing technologies. One of them is Oxford Nanopore Technologies. 

## Oxford Nanopore Technologies Origins

Oxford Nanopore Technologies, originally Oxford Nanolabs, was founded in 2005 by Dr Gordon Sanghera, Dr Spike Willcocks, and Professor Hagan Bayley. Professor David Dreamer theorized that individual DNA nucleotides passing through a protein channel in a lipid membrane could produce measurable changes in ionic current. This theory led to him teaming up with other professors to collaborate in experiments.

## Revenue

In 2005, it raised £500,000 from seed investors, primarily from the IP Group. In 2023, Oxford Nano Technologies reported revenue of £169.7 million, with growth up to between £180 million and £195 million.

## Main Business Focus

Oxford Nanopore Technologies is primarily focused on developing molecular sensing technology using nanopores for the direct, real-time sequencing of DNA and RNA. Through this, they strive to make sequencing accessible by providing research on genetics, cancers, diseases, etc. 

## Future Success

Oxford Nanopore Technologies is projected to continue growing and expanding, with expected revenue growth of over 30%. They are also expected to make their sequencing faster, label-free, and more accessible in the future.
