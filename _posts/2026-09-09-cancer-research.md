# A Dive into Kidney Cancer

For my cancer research, I wanted to focus on kidney cancer. At its core, kidney cancer occurs when the cells in the kidneys grow and divide at an unpredictable and rapid pace, which leads to these abnormal cells building up instead of dying; as a result of this buildup, a tumor forms.

## Frequency of Kidney Cancer

Each year, for every 100,000 men and women, there are 18 with kidney cancer. In 2026 alone, there have been an estimated 80,450 new cases. 

There are many variations of kidney cancer, such as transitional cell carcinoma, Wilms tumor, and kidney sarcomas. Commonly, those who have kidney cancer most likely have the variant called renal cell carcinoma (RCC), which affects the tubules, a part of the kidney that cleans the blood and makes urine. Among those with this, most are adults.

## How it is Identified/Detected

Kidney cancer can be identified if any abnormal activity occurs, such as a sudden fever, weakness, pain, or bloody urine. These symptoms can then be tested and diagnosed with blood, urine, or imaging tests. 

## Risk Factors

At the current moment, the development of kidney cancer is unclear. However, there are still many risk factors that could increase the chances of getting it. These factors do not guarantee the development or does it guareentee prevention of kidney disease if avoided, but should be considered, and be cautious regarding kidney cancer. Some of these factors are smoking, being overweight, high blood pressure, and older age.

## Stages

For kidney cancer, the TNM staging system is used. The T stands for the tumor and its size. In this area, there are 4 stages, ranging from T1 to T4. As one stage moves to another stage, the size of the tumor in the kidney increases. After the T stage, there is the N stage, standing for the lymph nodes and whether the cancer has spread to them. The N stage is split into N0 and N1, with N0 indicating no cancer cells in the nodes, while N1 indicates cancer cells are present. Lastly, the M stage stands for other parts of the body that the kidney cancer has spread to, with it having M0 and M1 as well. M0 indicates that it has not spread to other parts of the body, while M1 indicates that it has. 

Besides the TNM staging system, it can be numeric as well, ranging from 1-4, with 1 being the earliest and 4 the most advanced.

## Treatment

The treatment for kidney cancer includes nephrectomy, cryotherapy, microwave ablation (MVA), renal artery embolisation, and radiotherapy. Each of these treatments varies, as they correspond to the stage of the cancer to provide the most accurate and appropriate treatment. 

## Average Life Expectancy

It is estimated that those with kidney cancer, about 79.2% survives and can withstand the effects after 5 years. Age plays a big role in this statistic, as the younger the age, under 40, the survival rate is roughly 88% to 93%, but above 40, the percentage slowly decreases to around 61%.
